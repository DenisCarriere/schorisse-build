# Feasibility and Performance Notes

Status: **pre-design assumptions requiring professional verification**

## Planning approach

The concept assumes that rebuilding within the existing 17.0 × 7.5 m footprint
and a comparable pitched volume is the most plausible planning route. The design
retains the rural silhouette, terracotta roof character and masonry palette while
changing the openings and internal use.

This assumption is not a planning opinion. Confirm zoning, lawful existing use,
rebuild rights, residential use, floor-area treatment, parking, landscape impact,
heritage constraints and the required omgevingsvergunning with the municipality
and project architect.

## Information needed before design development

1. Measured building and topographical survey.
2. Boundary, easement and shared-lane information.
3. Existing-condition structural report.
4. Drainage, utility and ground investigation.
5. Confirmed orientation and solar context.
6. Planning pre-application advice.
7. Fire, accessibility and energy brief.

## Retention and access constraints

- Keep the shared tractor lane fully operational.
- Protect the pasture gate and boundary hedges.
- Retain the neighbouring brick building and narrow paved passage.
- Preserve the cobbled courtyard, curbs, clipped hedges, hydrangeas and mature
  trees as far as construction access allows.
- Develop a construction logistics plan for the constrained site.

## Baseline energy direction

The previous board assumed a Flemish near-zero-energy/E30-style baseline with:

- air-source heat pump and floor heating;
- balanced mechanical ventilation with heat recovery;
- airtight construction;
- high-performance triple glazing;
- approximately 6 kWp photovoltaic array;
- approximately 10,000 L rainwater recovery for toilets and garden.

All targets and equipment must be recalculated for the developed geometry and
the rules in force at application/construction.

## Certification options

The concept board compared LEED Silver, LEED Gold and certified Passivhaus. For a
single rural home in Belgium, performance-led design may be more useful than an
international commercial-building label. BREEAM, local EPB requirements and
Passivhaus may have different relevance depending on ownership, rental strategy
and project goals.

Possible performance measures include:

- airtightness testing and commissioning;
- thermal-bridge-free detailing;
- certified high-performance windows;
- low-VOC and EPD-backed materials;
- FSC or equivalent timber sourcing;
- reclaimed masonry where technically suitable;
- construction-waste sorting;
- retained permeable landscape and rainwater management.

## Glass-gable watch point

The approximately 33 m² glazed gable is the defining architectural move and the
largest envelope risk. The earlier board described it as north-facing, which
would reduce direct solar gain but increase heat-loss significance. Survey must
confirm orientation.

An energy model should test:

- whole-window Uw and glass Ug values;
- frame ratio and mullion sightlines;
- winter heat loss and interior surface temperatures;
- summer solar exposure and glare;
- airtightness at the portal/glazing junction;
- sliding-door threshold performance;
- condensation and ventilation strategy.

## Loft feasibility

The fixed 3.2 m eaves and 5.2 m ridge create a shallow approximately 28.1° roof.
The optional loft therefore has a narrow central standing band rather than full
second-floor headroom. Preliminary visual studies suggest approximately 10–18 m²
of comfortable standing area, with low storage/seating around it.

Feasibility depends on the real internal roof profile, required ceiling height
over bedrooms, structural floor depth, stair headroom, guarding, escape and fire
strategy. Do not treat the rendered loft as a measured room.
