# Structure and Envelope Concept

Status: **pre-design engineering brief**

This document describes a plausible structural and envelope strategy for design
coordination. It is not an engineer's design, calculation or specification.

## Existing building assumption

The early concept assumed full demolition of the target barn because its walls
and roof were understood to be structurally unsound. That condition must be
confirmed by a documented structural inspection. Reclaimed bricks may be sorted
for reuse even if the load-bearing fabric cannot be retained.

The adjacent side wing, neighbouring building and site boundaries are outside
the demolition target and require protection and coordinated junction details.

## Primary structural concept

- A reinforced concrete core cast within a proprietary insulated-concrete-form
  system forms the complete external wall shell, subject to the engineer's
  design and the selected manufacturer's system.
- Laminated-timber or steel roof members carry the roof and ridge and connect to
  the ICF wall shell through engineered bearing, tie-down and lateral details.
- The valley glass gable is non-structural glazing supported within the primary
  frame and a coordinated reinforced perimeter opening.
- The large sliding-door opening requires coordinated head, jamb, sill and frame
  deflection criteria from the structural and glazing designers.
- Foundations and slab must respond to survey, soil conditions, retained
  structures and any level changes.

## Roof geometry

| Item | Concept value |
|---|---:|
| Clear building width | 7.5 m external concept width |
| Eaves | 3.2 m |
| Ridge | 5.2 m |
| Roof rise | 2.0 m |
| Pitch derived from fixed geometry | approximately 28.1° |

The internal profile will be lower after rafters or panels, services, airtightness,
insulation and finishes. The architect and engineer must model the actual inner
roof line before fixing loft or stair levels.

## Wall and roof envelope

Concept palette:

- proprietary ICF forms, reinforced concrete core and system-specific bucks,
  lintels and connection zones;
- either a complete ventilated matte-black vertical fibre-cement rainscreen, or
  a hybrid rainscreen in which the living zone is fibre cement and the rear
  bedroom/service zone is a coordinated non-load-bearing Flemish brick veneer;
- deep black-lined window and door reveals that express the wall thickness while
  leaving every glazed opening clear;
- warm mineral-plaster interior finish, with a service strategy that protects the
  continuous wall shell;
- highly insulated pitched roof with a near-black standing-seam finish;
- continuous airtight layer and carefully modelled thermal bridges;
- dark-bronze external frames and smoked-oak entrance joinery.

The visual concept assumes approximately **35–45 cm overall external wall
build-up**, including the selected ICF form, concrete core, interior finish,
rainscreen cavity and selected cladding. The brick option may require a different
foundation ledge, substructure, cavity and overall depth from the fibre-cement
zone. Actual core thickness, reinforcement, insulation, fire performance,
fixing zones and total depth are manufacturer- and engineer-dependent design
variables, not fixed specifications.

## Glass gable

The visual concept shows an approximately 33 m² glazed field with:

- fixed triple-glazed panels;
- an approximately 3.0 m transom;
- triangular clerestory glazing;
- slim aluminium framing;
- one or two lift-slide door leaves;
- flush interior-to-exterior threshold.

The glass remains visually unobstructed. The wide opening requires an engineered
reinforced ICF perimeter, lintel or primary frame that controls wall, roof and
glazing loads without introducing screens or intermediate structure in front of
the glass. Deep returns must coordinate insulation continuity, waterproofing,
drainage, replacement access and frame anchorage.

Early targets were around Ug 0.5 W/m²K and, for a passive-level option, whole-frame
Uw at or below approximately 0.8 W/m²K. A façade consultant must confirm panel
sizes, safety glass, solar/thermal performance, drainage, movement, wind loads,
frame sightlines and replacement access.

The board previously described this gable as north-facing. A survey must confirm
the actual orientation before overheating and heat-loss conclusions are made.

## Partial loft structure

The optional loft sits over approximately 7–8.5 m of the rear private core.

- concept floor level: +2.40–2.45 m;
- central zone uses the available ridge height;
- low edges are non-standing storage/seating zones;
- stair approaches the loft near the ridge;
- front living hall remains open to the ridge.

The loft must be coordinated with portal spacing and should not introduce new
external walls, dormers or a higher ridge. Engineer design is required for floor
depth, vibration, acoustic separation, guarding, stair support and load transfer.

## Openings and thresholds

- Keep all doors and projections out of the shared tractor lane.
- Coordinate proprietary window and door bucks, reinforcement, fixings, flashings
  and cavity closers through the ICF/rainscreen build-up.
- Coordinate the recessed courtyard entrance with retained cobbles and drainage.
- Resolve level access at the glass gable without compromising weathering.
- Keep rooflights small, flush and within the rear loft zone if they remain in the
  developed design.

## Fire, acoustic and services coordination

The partial loft changes the fire and escape strategy. Design development must
confirm detection, escape travel, guarding, stair geometry, protected or
compartmented zones where required, and the relationship between open living
space and sleeping rooms.

Services should preserve the calm roof volume. Coordinate ventilation routes,
lighting, smoke detection, acoustic separation, kitchen extraction and any
sprinkler requirement before fixing exposed frames and roof finishes.

The ventilated fibre-cement facade requires coordinated cavity barriers, fire
classification, subframe fixings, base drainage, insect closures and replacement
details. The hybrid option additionally requires a designed masonry support,
cavity drainage, wall ties or support system and movement joints. Coordinate the
full-height metal flashing at the material change without interrupting drainage,
airtightness or insulation continuity. Confirm that all fixings transfer loads
into the designed structure and do not rely on the ICF facing alone.

## Engineer verification checklist

1. Existing condition and demolition sequence.
2. Ground investigation, foundations and retained-building movement.
3. ICF system, concrete core, reinforcement, openings, roof bearings and lateral
   stability.
4. Glazing support, deflection and wind load.
5. Loft floor, stair, vibration and guarding.
6. ICF/rainscreen/roof build-up, cladding fixings and thermal-bridge strategy.
7. Threshold drainage and water management.
8. Construction sequencing in the constrained courtyard and shared lane.
