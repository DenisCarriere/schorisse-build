01

Reading the site

Pacing the length
A 5′6″ (168 cm) person walks a natural pace of ~0.67–0.70 m. 25 paces ≈ 16.8–17.5 m — confirming a 17.0 m long volume. Existing barn depth read from roof proportion: ~7.5 m.
Planning note (Flanders)
Rebuilding on the same footprint and comparable volume is the safest permit route for a rural (often zone-foreign) barn. Keeping the pitched silhouette, terracotta roof and brick/timber palette supports the omgevingsvergunning.
02

Site plan — what stays, what changes

New construction — only the main barn is demolished and rebuilt, same footprint, ridge parallel to courtyard.
Retained buildings — attached side wing and neighbouring house are untouched; junction detailed with a slim expansion joint.
Retained landscape — yew cubes, trees, perimeter hedges and cobbles stay; they already frame the entrance perfectly.
Glass gable — the north-facing rear gable opens fully to lawn and valley: soft light, no overheating, big view.

08

Certification — Silver, Gold or Passivhaus?

Honest note first: LEED is rarely certified for single homes in Belgium — BREEAM dominates here, and every Flemish new build must already meet near-zero-energy rules (E-level ≤ E30), which overlaps heavily with LEED’s energy credits. The smart move: pick the performance tier first, then decide whether to pay for the plaque. All three tiers below are buildable by local firms — Passivhaus especially, since Brussels has required near-passive levels for all new builds since 2015 and Flanders has many certified passive/timber-frame builders.
EAAir-source heat pump + floor heating, balanced ventilation with heat recovery, airtightness ≤ 1.0 ACH, 6 kWp PV — the E30 package already scores most Energy & Atmosphere points.
MRRe-use of sorted bricks from the demolished barn, FSC laminated timber portal, construction-waste sorting plan.
WE10,000 L rainwater cistern feeding WCs and garden (standard Flemish requirement, easy LEED water points).
SS/LTRebuild on an existing footprint, retained hedges and trees, permeable cobbles — site credits come almost for free.
Premium over the E30 base build
LEED Silver (50–59 pts)+ €16k – 29k
Measures €10–20k + certification €6–9k · ≈3–6%. Mostly already in the base spec.
LEED Gold (60–79 pts)+ €27k – 45k
Better envelope, enhanced commissioning, low-VOC/EPD materials, extra water & waste credits €20–35k + certification €7–10k · ≈7–12%.
Certified Passivhaus+ €26k – 49k
Airtightness ≤0.6 ACH, thermal-bridge-free details, PH-certified glazing (Uw ≤ 0.8), PHPP design €20–40k + certification €6–9k · ≈7–13%. Heating drops to ≤15 kWh/m²·yr — a smaller heat pump partly pays it back.
Recommendation: Passivhaus if you keep or rent it long-term (local builders, real energy payback, EPC A+ rents faster); LEED Gold only if the plaque itself matters.
Design watch-point for Passivhaus: the 33 m² glass gable faces north — pure heat loss, no solar gain. It stays certifiable with Uw ≤ 0.8 frames and Ug ≈ 0.5 triple glazing, but PHPP will demand a thicker envelope elsewhere (≈35–40 cm walls, ≈45 cm roof). Budget the premium glazing inside the €32–45k gable line, not on top.
